Hollow Vanguard is a game about weight

Everything in it — the recoil, the reload, the camera lag — is tuned to one idea: that mass should be legible before it should be fun.

By Mira Halvorsen · 13 Min 2026 · 9 min read

The first thing Hollow Vanguard does is take away your hands. Not literally — the controls are ordinary — but the first ten minutes are spent proving that the character's body has opinions of its own. You ask for a stop and get a stop, two frames later, with a lean.

That lag reads as pretension in a demo and as authorship in a match. By the fourth hour you are not fighting the game; you are budgeting for it, the way you budget for a heavy bag on a long walk.

“We stopped asking whether an effect looked expensive and started asking whether it looked heavy.”

The technical story underneath is just as deliberate: an 8 ms frame budget that the art team treats as a design constraint, not a hardware limit. Every effect that survives the cut survives because it tells you something about your own weight.
