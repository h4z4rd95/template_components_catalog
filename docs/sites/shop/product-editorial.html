The Long Table

A reading on an object: what it is made of, why the joint is where it is, and what the surface does after five years. The buy panel stays with you, because a decision takes longer than a scroll.

One board, one decision

It starts as a single European oak board, air-dried for three years. Three years is not romance: below that, the board keeps moving after it is a table, and a table that moves is a table that gaps. The workshop buys once a year and builds from the stack in the order it was milled.

The table is not a product with a story attached. It is a story you can sit at.

The joint where you cannot see it

The apron meets the leg in a housed mortise, cut by hand and driven with a wedge. There is no screw in this table. That is not a boast about craft — it is the reason the leg can be tightened by a person with a mallet in ten years rather than replaced by a factory.

Oil, not lacquer

Finished with a hardwax oil so the surface can be repaired in place. A scratch is a local problem: sand one patch, oil it, and the repair ages into the rest within a season. Lacquer would have made that same scratch a decision about replacing the top.
